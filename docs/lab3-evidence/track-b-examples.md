## Failures

### F1: Bronze, invented "100 bonus points"

| Request | Profile |
|---|---|
| *Generate a retention offer for this Bronze member who shops in store, buys only apparel, and has not purchased in over two months.* | Bronze · churn 74% · 68 days since last purchase · LTV $312.80 · AOV $41.15 · online-to-store 0.20 · top category Apparel |

> **Subject:** We miss you—earn extra points on your next Apparel purchase
>
> **Body:** Hi there! We noticed it's been a couple of months since your last visit, and we'd love to welcome you back. As a Bronze member, you earn 1× points on every purchase, and we're offering you **100 bonus points** (worth $5) when you shop Apparel in the next 45 days—either online or at any of our 400 stores near you. That's a great way to build toward your next reward. Stop by or visit northstar.com today to claim your bonus.
>
> **Rationale:** This customer shops primarily in-store and in a single category (Apparel), so a points incentive tied to their core category and redeemable at physical locations is more relevant than shipping benefits; the 45-day window creates legitimate urgency without false scarcity.

| Faithfulness | Answer relevancy | Context recall |
|---|---|---|
| 0.059 | 0.528 | 0.600 |

Source: `track-b/offers_queryAB_interp_baseline_20261017T194547.md`. The same "100 bonus points" offer appears in baseline run 2 (`..._baseline_20261017T194651.md`).

**What went wrong / proposed fix:**

This email offers an incentive that doesn't exist in policy. It's tru that Bronze members earn 1x points on every purchase and that 100 points = $5, but we are not allowed to offer a 100-point gift. We told the model not to invent any promotions, but that is exactly what happened here. Luckily, the faithfulness score alerted us to the problem. To catch this in the future, it would be good to compare each benefit we're offering against that tier's allowed list right after generation. This way, we are not relying only on faithfulness metrics, and we can catch the error before the email is sent out.

### F2: Gold, offered 2× points (the Platinum earn rate)

| Request | Profile |
|---|---|
| *Generate a retention offer for this at-risk Gold member.* | Gold · churn probability 0.91 (from the Task 1 model) · top categories Home, Footwear, Beauty |

> **Subject:** Your Gold Rewards Are Waiting—Come Back for Extra Points
>
> **Body:** We've missed you! As a Gold member, you currently receive 1.5× points on every purchase, and we'd love to welcome you back with a special offer: earn **2× points on all Home and Footwear purchases for the next 45 days**. Whether you're shopping in-store or online, every dollar spent brings you closer to your next reward—and with your Gold status, you'll also enjoy free return shipping if you need it. Stop by your nearest NorthStar store or visit northstar.com today to start earning.
>
> **Rationale:** This offer leverages the customer's in-store preference (no shipping incentive needed), rewards their established Home and Footwear categories, uses a tier-appropriate benefit (elevated points), and anchors urgency to the 45-day validity window rather than artificial scarcity.

Not scored by RAGAS: this customer comes from the churn model's top at-risk list, not from the 4 harness test cases. Retrieved: 4. Tier Benefits; Loyalty; 2. Tiers; 1.1 Extended Window for Platinum Members; 5.1 Retention Offers; 5. Promotional Offers; 4. Non-Returnable Merchandise; Returns.

Source: `notebooks/lab3-demo.ipynb`, "From the model to an offer" cell (grounding rule on).

**What went wrong / proposed fix:**

Again, the model invented a promotion. Gold members only earn 1.5x points on purchases. This email offers them the Platinum rate without requiring the member to actually upgrade to a Platinum plan. This is a small error which makes it difficult to fix. The grounding rule didn't stop it because the email doesn't contain the specific promotional phrases that are listed in the grounding rule. We also didn't have a RAGAS evaluation on this email so there was no good way to catch it quickly. In the future, it would be good to pull out every number amount (dollars, points, etc) from the email and double check it against what the policies actually are to prevent the model from offering promotions that don't exist.

### S1: Platinum, leads with the $100 annual gear credit

| Request | Profile |
|---|---|
| *Generate a retention offer for this high-value Platinum member with broad category interest and a large average order.* | Platinum · churn 66% · 39 days since last purchase · LTV $8,940.00 · AOV $447.00 · online-to-store 0.55 · top categories Outdoor, Electronics, Home |

> **Subject:** Your $100 Gear Credit Expires December 31 — Platinum Exclusive
>
> **Body:** As a Platinum member, you have an exclusive $100 annual gear credit waiting to be used on outdoor, electronics, home, or any category in our store—and it expires December 31. This credit is yours to spend on exactly what you need, whether you're gearing up for your next adventure or refreshing your space at home. Since you shop both online and in our stores, you can redeem it wherever is most convenient. Visit northstar.com or stop by any of our 400 locations before year-end to make the most of this Platinum benefit.
>
> **Rationale:** This offer leverages the customer's highest-value tier benefit (the $100 annual credit), creates legitimate urgency tied to a real calendar deadline, spans all three of their top categories, and honors their omnichannel behavior without inventing a discount or overstating benefits.

| Faithfulness | Answer relevancy | Context recall |
|---|---|---|
| 0.143 | 0.246 | 0.333 |
